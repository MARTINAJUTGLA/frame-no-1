How to notice colour in films

A simple viewing guide using Pulp Fiction as a starting point.

By FRAME No. 1 · AI-assisted sample article · 19 November 2026 · 2 min read

Sample article written with ChatGPT/Codex assistance for this university project. Viewing observations are editorial interpretation.

Look at the whole scene

Colour is one of the first things we notice in a film, even before we think about it. Start by looking at the background, the lighting and the clothes together. In the dance scene from Pulp Fiction, the blue lighting and dark surroundings make the dancers’ black-and-white clothes stand out. You do not need to know technical terms to notice that contrast.

Compare two moments

Choose a colour and watch for it again. Does it appear in a different location or on another character? Notice whether the lighting changes between indoor and outdoor scenes. There is no universal rule that every colour must mean the same thing. Describe what you can actually see before deciding what it might suggest.

Try this on your next film

Pick one scene you remember clearly. Write down its main colours, where the light comes from and which person or object attracts your eye first. Then watch that scene again. You may notice that the image directs your attention differently from the dialogue. Add one sentence to your film notes about what you discovered.
